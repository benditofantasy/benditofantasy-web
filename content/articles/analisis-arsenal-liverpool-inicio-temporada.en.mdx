Hi, friends.

We are back with the second installment of our team-by-team analysis ahead of the start of the season. This time, we turn our attention to Arsenal and Liverpool, the Premier League's two most recent champions, although they arrive at the new campaign under rather different circumstances.

On one side, we have Arsenal, strengthened by winning the Premier League last season and by their emphatic Community Shield victory over Manchester City last weekend. On the other, Liverpool begin a new adventure under Andoni Iraola following Arne Slot's relatively short tenure. It will be fascinating to see what the Spaniard can do with this team and, more importantly for us, what this change could mean from a Fantasy perspective.

Because if Arsenal and Liverpool have one thing in common, it is that they always offer plenty of potential for our FPL teams. The question is figuring out which players we should actually consider and, perhaps just as importantly, which potential traps we should avoid.

As with the previous article, I listened to the interviews with both clubs' correspondents from Planet FPL's Correspondent Week series, where they went into considerable detail about their squads and what we might expect from them this season. I encourage you to listen to both episodes in full, but for now, these are some of the conclusions I took away and how they might help us build our teams.

## Arsenal

Arsenal begin the season as champions and once again as one of the leading contenders to win the title. For us, however, the question is where to invest within a squad that now offers an enormous number of alternatives.

### Three players to pick

**Christos Tzolis — £6.5m**

The first candidate making a very strong case is Christos Tzolis. He has only just arrived at the club, but he looked very good during preseason and costs just £6.5m. At the moment, he can probably be considered one of Arsenal's best attacking options to begin the season, even ahead of much more established names such as Bukayo Saka or Kai Havertz.

With Trossard gone, there is also less competition for his position, making it likely that he will see significant minutes during the opening gameweeks. Adam Pritchard, Arsenal's Planet FPL correspondent, even identified him as his preferred Arsenal attacker to begin the season and highlighted his involvement in some set pieces.

The Community Shield strengthened that case even further, with Tzolis recording two assists against Manchester City.

> **At £6.5m, Tzolis could be one of the best routes into the champions' attack.**

There is, of course, something we need to watch. He is a new player, and we need some certainty around his minutes before viewing him as a long-term option. If he starts coming off early or we begin seeing signs of increased rotation, we will have to reassess. For now, though, he looks very interesting.

**Riccardo Calafiori — £5.5m**

The second name is another player who enjoyed a strong preseason and also scored in the Community Shield: Riccardo Calafiori.

At £5.5m, he gives us access to the defensive line of the best defensive team in the league while potentially being one of Arsenal's most attack-minded defenders from a Fantasy perspective. He can push forward, arrive in advanced positions and create opportunities, giving him routes to points beyond clean sheets alone.

His attacking potential appears greater than Mosquera's and, while Gabriel will always carry that enormous threat from corners, his price is beginning to become prohibitive and will probably keep him out of many of our teams. Pritchard also showed a clear preference for Calafiori among Arsenal's cheaper defensive options and was quite confident about his chances of starting the opening gameweek.

If we want Arsenal defensive coverage without committing too much of our budget, Calafiori looks like one of the best options.

**Kai Havertz — £7.5m**

The next player surprises me a little, but his candidacy is supported by his consistency, the correspondent's assessment and even what we heard on the latest episode of Bendito Fantasy, where Profe mentioned him as one of the interesting alternatives in attack.

Kai Havertz costs £7.5m and still appears to be one of Arteta's preferred players, even with Gyökeres in the squad. One of his greatest advantages is precisely his ability to adapt to different structures. He does not have to play exclusively as the number nine to stay in the team, which gives him several different routes to minutes.

Havertz can also contribute as a link in the buildup rather than simply appearing as the player who finishes chances. Pritchard preferred him to Gyökeres as well and, at the time the episode was recorded, even had Havertz in his own Fantasy team.

He might not be the most obvious name when we think about Arsenal's attack, but at £7.5m there are enough arguments to take him seriously.

### One player to monitor

**Martin Ødegaard — £6.5m**

Martin Ødegaard is one of the players to monitor. His £6.5m price is interesting, although Arsenal now have an enormous number of alternatives in midfield.

What really interests me is his positioning. Ødegaard finished last season well and has appeared intent on playing further forward during preseason.

If that advanced positioning continues and begins translating into returns, £6.5m could quickly become a very attractive price. Before committing to him, however, I want to see his minutes and, above all, how consistently he can produce Fantasy points.

### One player to overlook

**Bruno Guimarães — £7.0m**

One of Arsenal's new faces this season is Bruno Guimarães. We know perfectly well how talented he is, what he can create and how much control he can bring to a midfield, but being an outstanding Premier League footballer and being an outstanding Fantasy asset are two different things.

Bruno can play as a six or alongside Declan Rice, and that is precisely where the problem begins. At Arsenal, he may not enjoy the same attacking freedom he had at Newcastle. Pritchard does not consider him a particularly attractive option to begin with either and believes his function within Arsenal's new midfield could limit his Fantasy potential.

At £7.0m, slightly more expensive than Ødegaard and potentially operating in a deeper role, there are simply too many alternatives I would rather own. For now, he stays off our list.

## Liverpool

Liverpool enter a new era under Andoni Iraola, and that alone makes them one of the most interesting teams to watch during the opening gameweeks.

The idea appears to be a return to much more aggressive football, built around a high press and with Florian Wirtz moving close to Alexander Isak when Liverpool try to regain possession. Lauren Harris, Liverpool's correspondent, expects a team playing with far greater intensity, but she also warned that this approach could produce much more open matches while the players adjust to the system.

> **Liverpool could become one of the most attractive teams in Fantasy precisely because their matches might be much more open.**

From an attacking Fantasy perspective, that interests us enormously.

### Three players to pick

**Alexander Isak — £9.0m**

Alexander Isak is an excellent candidate, although his £9.0m price represents a significant investment.

His biggest advantage is quite simple: he is Liverpool's starting number nine. With little natural competition for the position, he is likely to accumulate plenty of minutes, although obviously his fitness will remain important. Staying healthy has repeatedly been one of his biggest obstacles.

There have been encouraging signs during preseason, and Harris particularly highlighted the connection he is beginning to develop with Florian Wirtz. She is also starting the season with Isak in her own team.

There is plenty of confidence in him, and this could perfectly well be another season in which we see him fighting near the top of the goalscoring charts, just as we became accustomed to seeing at Newcastle.

**Florian Wirtz — £7.5m**

The second player we should strongly consider is Florian Wirtz.

Here we find something different from what we saw last season. Iraola appears prepared to use him in a position much closer to his ideal role. He operates as the number ten and, when Liverpool press, moves almost alongside Isak. That means that whenever Liverpool regain possession high up the pitch, Wirtz should immediately find himself in exactly the kind of position from which he can create a chance.

Harris also highlighted his involvement on corners and reached a conclusion that could prove controversial: Wirtz may actually have a higher Fantasy ceiling than Dominik Szoboszlai.

That is a significant statement considering the consistency we have seen from the Hungarian, but Wirtz's positioning within Iraola's new system makes him well worth considering.

**Dominik Szoboszlai — £7.0m**

And that brings us directly to the third player: Dominik Szoboszlai.

He still has many of the qualities that make him tremendously interesting for Fantasy. He is involved in set pieces, possesses that extraordinary long-range shot and, perhaps most importantly, offers considerable security of minutes. His versatility should keep him consistently involved in Iraola's team even if some of the pieces around him change.

Harris is beginning the season with both Wirtz and Szoboszlai, although if forced to choose only one she would take the German because of his greater attacking ceiling.

Szoboszlai is the kind of player who will probably become a fixture in many teams, particularly given Liverpool's favorable opening schedule. It seems difficult to begin without any of their attacking players, and there are perfectly reasonable arguments for owning more than one.

### One player to monitor

**Milos Kerkez — £5.5m**

Our player to monitor comes from the defense: Milos Kerkez.

We also heard his name on this week's Bendito Fantasy podcast, where Profe mentioned him as a possible way into the Liverpool defense at a relatively low price while retaining interesting attacking potential.

Harris herself acknowledges that Kerkez has been heavily involved going forward from left-back. The problem is not necessarily Kerkez. It is Liverpool. We need to discover how solid Iraola's team will actually be defensively and whether they can produce enough clean sheets to make us want to invest in this back line.

With that uncertainty, if we eventually want a Liverpool defender, entering through a £5.5m player with attacking upside may prove considerably more attractive than paying for one of the premium alternatives.

### One player to overlook

**Virgil van Dijk — £6.5m**

And those defensive concerns are precisely why Virgil van Dijk is the Liverpool player we are leaving out.

Not because he is a bad Fantasy player. Quite the opposite. He has several routes to points: potential clean sheets, DefCon and, of course, the threat he poses from every corner. The problem is his price.

Harris was particularly pessimistic about Liverpool's defense and believes that, while the team learns to execute Iraola's aggressive press, they could concede opportunities against almost anyone. Even so, if she had to select a Liverpool defender, Van Dijk would remain the most reliable option.

But at £6.5m, we need more than reliability. We need production that justifies the investment, and while cheaper routes into the same defense exist, that extra million can probably be put to better use elsewhere.

Arsenal and Liverpool will once again be major players this season, and several of these names will almost certainly find their way into our teams at some point. As always, the key is not simply finding the best footballer, but finding the best footballer for Fantasy. And between Tzolis, Calafiori, Havertz, Isak, Wirtz and Szoboszlai, we have several interesting places to start.
