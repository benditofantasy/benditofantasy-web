Do you remember that season when everything seemed to go your way in Fantasy? Your captain delivered, your transfers paid off, and you finished in a position you've struggled to match ever since. What happened next? Have you managed another season like that, or have you also experienced campaigns where nothing seems to work out?

In football, having the best squad doesn't guarantee trophies. Fantasy works much the same way: you can use the best models, study statistics, and prepare for every Gameweek down to the smallest detail, yet still finish nowhere near your goals. Effort improves our chances, but it doesn't guarantee good results. There will always be injuries, unexpected rotations, and decisions that don't work out, no matter how carefully we analyze them.

> Effort improves our chances, but it doesn't guarantee good results.

That makes it worth asking ourselves what we expect to achieve in Fantasy and how much we're willing to invest to get there. Do we want to compete for a Top 10k finish, win our mini-league, or simply enjoy a hobby we spend a few minutes on each week?

Sometimes our expectations don't match the way we play. Other times, even when we've done everything within our control, we struggle to accept that results don't depend entirely on us.

## Six Ways to Play Fantasy

In his book *177 Mental Toughness Secrets of the World Class*, Steve Siebold describes five levels related to mental toughness and how people approach competition and success. Taking his model as a starting point, we've adapted those ideas to Fantasy and developed six profiles representing different ways of approaching the game, from managers who play out of habit to those pursuing ambitious goals.

This isn't a classification of good and bad managers, nor is it a ladder we're necessarily supposed to climb. These are different motivations we may recognize in ourselves, and they can even change over the course of a season.

## The Six Profiles

### 1. Playing on Autopilot

There are thirty minutes left before the deadline. You open the app, replace an injured player, adjust your starting eleven, switch captains at the last minute, and close it. Another Gameweek sorted.

You haven't followed the news or checked the upcoming fixtures. You simply keep playing because you've been doing it for several seasons and Fantasy has become part of your routine. You're not necessarily bored with it; the game just isn't particularly high on your list of priorities.

### 2. Playing Not to Lose

There are four Gameweeks left, and you're 35 points ahead in your mini-league. Everyone is transferring in the in-form striker you don't own. Do you bring him in too? Perhaps you'd rather do that than risk seeing one big performance wipe out your lead.

Your priority is protecting what you've already achieved. In certain situations, limiting risk and holding your position can matter more than trying to gain a significant advantage.

### 3. Playing to Cruise

The deadline is approaching, and you need to choose your captain. You check a recommendation, look at the fixtures, and compare a couple of options. You haven't studied the statistics or followed every press conference, but you have enough information to make a decision.

You want to compete and get good results, but you're not willing to spend much more time on Fantasy. You prefer to make use of the resources available and give the game what you consider a reasonable amount of your time.

### 4. Playing to Improve

Your captain scores two points. You picked him based on his form, his opponent, and his chances of scoring. Rather than focusing solely on the outcome, you revisit the alternatives you had considered. Did you interpret the data correctly? Did you overlook something?

You're interested in learning from your decisions, recognizing mistakes, and developing your own judgment. You understand that a good decision doesn't always translate into a good score.

> We don't all play Fantasy with the same goals, or put in the same effort to achieve them.

### 5. Playing to Compete

Your mini-league is incredibly tight, with only a few points separating you from the managers at the top. You're no longer looking only at which players might score the most points. You're also looking for the ones who could give you an edge over your rivals.

You study their squads, follow their moves, and look for differentials. You want to improve your team, but you also want to climb the standings and finish ahead of the people you're competing against.

### 6. Playing to Win

Your goal is a Top 1k finish. It's not a passing ambition after a good Gameweek; it's your main motivation, and it shapes the way you approach the season. You plan transfers, study different scenarios, compare projections, and evaluate how to use your chips.

You're willing to invest the time and effort needed to reach every deadline as well prepared as possible. You know that pursuing a goal like this requires ambition, discipline, and consistency, even though the best preparation in the world can't guarantee you'll achieve what you set out to do.

## And a Bonus: Going Beyond

Our relationship with Fantasy doesn't end with the decisions we make each Gameweek. Some managers also find satisfaction in sharing what they learn, exchanging ideas, or contributing something to others.

Maybe you share information and analysis in your WhatsApp group, help a friend understand a rule, or join conversations about matches and upcoming Gameweeks. Or perhaps you create content, run a social media account, or get together with a coworker to record a weekly podcast.

You've spent weeks working on a model to project points for the next Gameweek. After testing it and fixing its flaws, you decide to share it with other managers. Someone uses it, makes a suggestion, or develops an idea based on your work.

Fantasy becomes a space for creating, experimenting, and sharing. It doesn't matter whether you do it with a handful of friends or a larger community. Your satisfaction no longer depends exclusively on your team's results, but also on what you can contribute to others.

This motivation doesn't replace any of the previous six profiles. You can pursue a great overall rank, compete for your mini-league, or simply enjoy the game without spending too much time on it, while also finding satisfaction in sharing your experience. Going beyond isn't a higher level; it's another way of experiencing Fantasy that can exist alongside any of the others.

## Expectations and Reality

After exploring these six profiles, we may notice contradictions between our expectations and the way we play. Perhaps you're aiming for a great overall rank but barely spend a few minutes preparing for each Gameweek. Or, on the other hand, you invest considerable time and resources trying to win, yet struggle to accept that all that effort doesn't guarantee the outcome you're hoping for.

Recognizing our profile helps us bring our expectations in line with reality. If we play on autopilot, we need to ask ourselves whether expecting a Top 10k finish is realistic given that level of commitment. If we play to win, we need to understand that even the best preparation doesn't guarantee victory. In either case, we need to be clear about what we can control and what lies beyond our control.

> No matter how passionate we are about it, it's still just a game.
