Hi, friends. This is the third installment of these team analysis articles and, with it, I am wrapping up this short series focused on the Big Six. Although, as you may have noticed, I am deliberately replacing Tottenham with Aston Villa. Part of the reason is simple: over the last few years, Villa have looked more like a Big Six club than Spurs. But there is also another reason. Aston Villa are going through a transition that I find particularly interesting from a Fantasy perspective.

Manchester City and Aston Villa are obviously two very good teams, but both arrive at this season at an important point of change. City begin their first campaign after Pep Guardiola. Enzo Maresca knows the club, has already been part of its structure and brings a recognizable footballing base, but that does not mean we know exactly what to expect. In fact, after what we saw in the Community Shield, predicting the starting XI feels considerably more difficult than we would like. Even so, City have an attractive schedule and still offer some enormous certainties. One of them, quite literally, is the most expensive player in the entire game.

Aston Villa are going through an even more radical transition. They arrive after winning a European trophy, but also after one of the biggest player raids I can remember in recent years. Morgan Rogers, Youri Tielemans and Lucas Digne are gone, and Ezri Konsa has now joined Arsenal. Amadou Onana is also out for the entire season, while there have even been changes within Unai Emery’s coaching staff. And perhaps the departures are not over yet. Ollie Watkins has attracted interest from Saudi Arabia and the striker does not appear opposed to the idea of leaving. Losing him would be another major blow for a team that has accustomed us to competing above what we might historically have expected from them.

So yes, these are two strong teams. But they are also two teams where the scale of change forces us to look beyond the badge. Let’s talk about the players.

## Manchester City

### Three players to pick

**1. Erling Haaland — £15.5m**

The first recommendation could not be anyone else. The face of the team, the goalscorer and the most expensive player in the game: Erling Haaland. Almost all of us own him, except perhaps the bravest managers, and our correspondent described him as the player who almost removes every other question. At the time of the conversation, he had more than 70% ownership, he starts at home against Bournemouth and he is the leading captaincy candidate not just for GW1, but probably for many Gameweeks throughout the season.

The problem, obviously, is the £15.5m price tag, because owning Haaland means building a large part of your squad around him. The only real concern could be his fitness going into the opening weekend, but I consider that a fairly minor issue. Haaland is normally in outstanding physical condition and, after a couple of weeks back in training, he should be ready enough to do what we always expect from him: score goals.

On [this week’s Bendito Fantasy episode](https://www.youtube.com/live/_A2G8BDknZ4?si=cv0c1CWFwwf2MyDG), our guest Pepe del Bosque summarized the entire Haaland debate very well. The question we should ask ourselves is simple:

> **How many goals do we think Haaland will score in the first five Gameweeks?**

The answer probably tells us whether we should own him or not.

**2. Antoine Semenyo — £8.5m**

The second recommendation is Antoine Semenyo. And this one is interesting because picking Manchester City players beyond Haaland usually becomes a headache. Semenyo, however, seems to have enough going for him to find his way into plenty of squads this season. The first signal has been his preseason. Maresca is asking him to provide width and aggression in one-on-one situations, which is exactly where we know he is strong. Our correspondent expects him to play a lot of minutes and believes City will need significant attacking production from him. Doku’s injury increases that responsibility even further.

Semenyo could become one of Haaland’s main partners, not just in terms of goals, but also in creating the final pass. A significant part of City’s attacking output could end up running through those two players. The key Fantasy question is what that width under Maresca really means. If it keeps Semenyo too far away from goal, his shot volume could suffer. If that width still comes with regular box entries, then we may be looking at one of the most interesting midfielders to begin the season.

**3. Phil Foden — £7.0m**

The third recommendation comes with an asterisk. Phil Foden costs only £7.0m, which is an extremely attractive price if we ever get back the version of Foden we know from his best seasons. There has been plenty of talk about Maresca seeing him as one of the central pieces of this new City side. The problem is that we still need to see it. Rayan Cherki is significant competition as well. One strong performance from the Frenchman could quickly change the hierarchy and turn Foden into another one of those City players capable of giving us a big return one week and leaving us wondering whether he will even start the next.

So while I am keeping him among the recommendations because of his price and upside, he is probably the City recommendation that requires the most caution. At £7.0m, however, that risk becomes considerably easier to accept.

### One player to monitor

**Nico O’Reilly — £6.5m**

Nico O’Reilly is expensive for a defender, but he is also a very unusual defender. His background as an attacking player helps explain how naturally he arrives in advanced areas and inside the box. The big question over the summer was whether that freedom would survive the managerial change, and the Community Shield gave us an encouraging first answer. Once again, we saw him attacking space and appearing high up the pitch, exactly the kind of behavior that could make him Manchester City’s most attractive defender in Fantasy.

There is still some uncertainty, though. Maresca may alter which full-back pushes forward depending on the opponent, and there is plenty of competition within the squad. That is why I am not ready to pay £6.5m yet. But if the opening Gameweeks confirm that O’Reilly keeps that attacking freedom and City begin to find more defensive stability, he will become very difficult to ignore.

### One player to avoid

**Elliot Anderson — £6.5m**

This one will probably create some discussion. Elliot Anderson was one of the most interesting players last season and plenty of managers will immediately think about his defensive contributions. But the context has changed. Rodri has now left Manchester City for Barcelona, and that creates one very important question around Anderson: **where exactly is he going to play?** Our correspondent had already warned, before Rodri’s departure was completed, that Anderson could end up operating in a deeper midfield role. There were also doubts about how much of his defensive contribution output he could maintain while playing for a team expected to dominate possession.

That question is now even more relevant. If Maresca uses him mainly as a deeper midfielder, his Fantasy appeal at £6.5m drops considerably. If he plays higher up, then we can reconsider. As a Fantasy option, I have doubts. As a footballer and a Manchester City signing, none.

## Aston Villa

Aston Villa require a different approach. As I mentioned at the start, the squad has been almost completely reshaped over the summer and they also begin with Brighton away followed by Arsenal in GW2. That does not make them especially appealing as an early investment, but it gives us something valuable: **time**. We can use those first Gameweeks to study the new Villa, understand which pieces remain important after such a major transition, and then reassess once the fixtures improve.

### Three players to pick

**1. Ollie Watkins — £8.0m**

As long as he remains an Aston Villa player, Ollie Watkins is my first recommendation. He has scored 16 goals in each of the last two seasons and finished the previous campaign particularly well. Our correspondent considers £8.0m a very reasonable price for a striker with that level of production. We have already discussed elsewhere that the ideal forward structure this season may involve one premium striker alongside cheaper options. Naturally, that makes it harder to find room for Watkins. But if you want to invest more heavily up front, he offers something relatively few forwards can provide: consistency.

And unlike Haaland, Watkins is not part of the current template, which also gives him differential appeal. All of this comes with one obvious condition: **if he stays**. The interest from Saudi Arabia is real, and losing him would completely change both this recommendation and what we can expect from Aston Villa in attack.

**2. Matty Cash — £4.5m**

The second recommendation is a familiar name: Matty Cash. Villa’s defenders have dropped in price, and that reopens a conversation we probably would not be having if they were still priced at £5.0m. The issue is familiar as well: defensive contributions have not traditionally been a major strength for these players. Cash, however, has one way of compensating for that. He attacks. His forward runs and his goal and assist threat make him, in our correspondent’s view, the most interesting Villa defender for Fantasy.

That does not mean we need to buy him immediately. Konsa’s departure weakens a defense that already raises questions, so clean sheets could be even less reliable this season. But at £4.5m, we do not need to play him every week. We can pick the fixtures.

**3. Emi Buendía — £6.0m**

And now we get to one of those players we seem to have been waiting on for years. Emi Buendía has talent, upside and the ability to produce excellent moments. What he still has not consistently produced is regularity. Last season he scored six goals in just 21 starts, and our correspondent Lee currently considers him the best Aston Villa midfielder for Fantasy. I agree. Given what Villa have left, he is probably the midfielder who interests me most.

He offers direct goal threat and, at least initially, appears to have an advantage over players like Garnacho and Manzambi. At £6.0m, this is a gamble, but not a particularly expensive one. If Buendía finally turns his good spells into sustained production, he could become a very useful differential.

### One player to monitor

**Johan Manzambi — £6.0m**

And that brings us directly to Manzambi, who is the player I want to keep under the microscope. He is only 20 years old and comes off a very good World Cup, where he produced three goals and two assists. The talent is obvious, but there is a difference between recognizing talent and buying the promise immediately. He still has to adapt to a new country, a demanding system under Emery and a squad that itself is changing around him.

There is no need to get ahead of ourselves. Let’s first see his minutes, his position and the level of trust Emery gives him. If those answers are encouraging, then we can start having a serious conversation about Manzambi.

> **Recognizing talent is one thing. Buying the promise immediately is another.**

### One player to avoid

**Emiliano Martínez — £5.0m**

Finally, we arrive at Emiliano Martínez, and this is beginning to feel like the end of an era. Villa have already moved to bring in another goalkeeper and everything points toward a transition between the posts. But even setting that aside, £5.0m is difficult to justify. Our correspondent was quite clear: at £4.5m there would be a conversation; at £5.0m, there is not. Especially when we can access the same defense through £4.5m players who, like Matty Cash, also offer some attacking upside.

Martínez has given us some excellent Fantasy seasons in the past. This time, his price and Aston Villa’s circumstances make him an easy avoid.

## Two transitions, two reasons to wait

So there you have it, friends. This is the third installment of our team analysis series and we are now very close to the start of the season. Manchester City and Aston Villa arrive from very different positions, but both force us to do something that is often difficult in Fantasy: **wait for information**. At City, we can buy the biggest certainties while we figure out how Maresca intends to organize all the pieces at his disposal. At Villa, we can identify the interesting prices now, but allow those difficult opening fixtures to show us what is really left of Emery’s side after such a dramatic summer of change.

I hope this series has helped bring a little more clarity to your selections, particularly around those bigger clubs where we are constantly searching for that final piece to complete our squads. Good luck, and I’ll see you in the mini-league.
