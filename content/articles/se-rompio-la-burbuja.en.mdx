The season has barely started, and we are already beginning to find out how much some of the certainties we built during preseason were really worth.

We spent weeks talking about fixtures, prices, transfers, structures, captaincy options and apparently easy matches. We built our teams around those ideas and, almost without realizing it, created a bubble. One in which some games seemed decided before they were played and certain teams appeared reliable simply because of the badge on their shirt.

It only took the opening matches of the season to start breaking it.

Arsenal began their campaign to defend the title by doing exactly what we expected. **A 3-0 win over Coventry City**, a newly promoted side that offered little resistance against the champions of England and runners-up in Europe. There, the preseason script survived: there was a clear favorite, they behaved like one, and for now at least, some of our certainties remain intact.

Then Hull City arrived.

## The Easy Fixture That Never Existed

For many managers, Manchester United’s trip to Hull looked like one of the easiest fixtures of the opening part of the season. United were clear favorites and, from a Fantasy perspective, that had made several of their players very attractive options. Some managers even saw enough upside to hand the captaincy to **Bruno Fernandes or Bryan Mbeumo**.

Ninety minutes later, Manchester United were heading home after a **2-0 defeat**, and Hull had delivered the first major shock of the season.

But reducing it to the scoreline would miss the most important part.

Hull played with intensity, personality and an aggression that Manchester United never managed to solve. They defended with discipline when necessary and, when the opportunities arrived, particularly from set pieces, they took them. United had the ball for long stretches, but having possession and doing something useful with it turned out to be two very different things.

The first bubble had burst.

Not only because Manchester United lost, but because Hull immediately stopped looking like the newly promoted side we could simply target with our Fantasy teams.

And with that, new names entered the notebook.

**Nobel Mendy** finished as one of the biggest scorers of the Gameweek with **15 points**, combining a goal, a clean sheet and bonus. **McBurnie** also picked up an assist, and Hull suddenly began to offer something we had not spent much time considering during preseason: cheap players inside a team that may be considerably more competitive than expected.

But perhaps the most interesting Fantasy name came from the goalkeeper.

**Tzolakis**, with only **0.6% ownership and a price of £4.5 million**, finished with **10 points**, five saves and maximum bonus. One match obviously does not turn him into a recommendation, but it is enough to bring him into the conversation.

That is exactly what the opening Gameweek should do: not force us to change our opinions immediately, but give us information we did not have before.

The same logic applies to **Egan**. At just **£4.0 million**, he recorded **21 defensive contributions**. Mendy and Belohoumi also crossed the threshold required to score through that route. Hull are beginning to show us that there may be cheap defenders capable of producing something even when the clean sheets disappear, although that does not mean we should suddenly trust this defense to deliver shutouts consistently.

And this is where the excitement needs to be controlled.

Hull travel to Coventry City in Gameweek 2, precisely one of the fixtures many of us had identified during preseason as favorable for Coventry and even potentially useful for Bench Boost strategies. After what happened this weekend, that assumption deserves another look.

But after that come **Aston Villa, Chelsea and an away trip to Newcastle**.

> **One great match changes the information available to us. It should not immediately change every decision we make.**

## Brentford Break Another Certainty

If Hull delivered the first blow, Brentford finished the job of reminding us that the Premier League has very little respect for our preseason plans.

Tottenham lost **3-0** away to a Brentford side that did almost everything Spurs could not: they played with clarity, intensity and personality.

Goals from **Lewis-Potter, Kayode and Janelt** built the scoreline, but one of the names we had already been highlighting as potentially important this season immediately appeared: **Mamadou Sangaré**.

Two assists, three bonus points and a performance that demands attention.

At **£5.5 million**, Sangaré could become one of the standout value picks of the season if what we saw in this first match proves sustainable. The upcoming schedule also gives us a little more reason to watch closely: **Leeds, Sunderland, Bournemouth and Chelsea** are Brentford’s next four opponents.

Again, that does not mean you have to buy him tonight.

It means we now have genuine reasons to keep watching him.

There were interesting signs around him too. **Senesi** maintained the strong defensive-contribution numbers we already associated with him, reaching **15**, while **Robertson** recorded 11. On the Brentford side, Janelt, Sangaré, Ajer and Schade also crossed the defensive-contribution threshold.

There is Fantasy information everywhere, but the collective picture may be even more important.

Brentford looked like a serious football team.

Tottenham did not. At least not yet.

## Names Do Not Play Football

It would be absurd to write off Manchester United or Tottenham after one match.

Both are going through adaptation processes. There are new players, important absences and systems that still need time. Michael Carrick and Roberto De Zerbi have plenty of work ahead, and we will almost certainly see much better versions of these teams as the season progresses.

But Gameweek 1 has already given them a warning.

Manchester United could not turn up at Hull expecting the difference in reputation to win the match for them, and Tottenham could not assume that the money spent during the summer would automatically transform a collection of talented footballers into a good team.

Hull and Brentford did something much simpler.

They played serious football.

One found intensity and personality in front of its own crowd; the other showed structure and clarity against an opponent that still looks under construction. The two favorites had the bigger names, the higher expectations and probably more players inside our Fantasy squads.

The points went to the other teams.

## The First Lesson of the Season

The temptation after a Gameweek like this will be to react.

Buy the player who scored 15 points. Sell the one who gave us one or two. Tear apart structures we spent weeks building because 90 minutes contradicted our expectations.

That would also be a mistake.

Gameweek 1 serves a different purpose.

It is the first moment of the season when we stop working exclusively with projections and begin receiving real information. We discover roles, structures, set-piece specialists, defensive contributions, minutes and collective behaviors that no color-coded fixture ticker could show us before the ball actually started moving.

> **The opening Gameweek should not force us to change our minds immediately. It should give us information we did not have before.**

That is why **Tzolakis, Egan and, especially, Sangaré** now enter our conversation. Mendy was one of the standout performers of the Gameweek, but chasing his 15 points would be confusing one excellent performance with a sufficiently strong Fantasy signal.

They have done enough to make us look.

---

### Final Whistle

The teams that looked destined to be this Gameweek’s victims are showing us the real value of stability. While several members of the Big Six made major changes and spent huge amounts of money during the summer, teams with greater continuity are producing some of the strongest performances.

Hull City and Brentford are the first warning signs. Perhaps we entered the season too focused on new names, major transfers and the teams that changed the most, when some of the best Fantasy opportunities may come precisely from those who changed the least.

Their players now have to enter our conversation.

**Who has caught your attention so far?**
