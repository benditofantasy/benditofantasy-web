Friends, the season has officially begun.

The Fantasy Towers opened their doors to the 2026–27 season on Thursday, and many of us immediately rushed to build our first drafts and secure an ID that would not be too difficult to remember.

With that first squad now saved in the app, the questions begin. The names, prices and star players start moving into place.

And that leads me to a reflection I usually have at the beginning of every season: **who is the first player you add when your squad is completely blank?**

Those who have followed Bendito Fantasy for a long time know this is not a new question. We have asked it to several champions after the season ends, and the answers have varied considerably. That makes sense. Every manager follows a slightly different strategy.

But one answer comes up more often than the others.

A premium player.

For many years, FPL had that mystical player, almost a unicorn, capable of occupying the category we know as premium. It was not simply that he was expensive. He was the player around whom we built the entire team.

The cornerstone.

Iconic names immediately come to mind: Thierry Henry, Cristiano Ronaldo, Frank Lampard, Sergio Agüero, Alexis Sánchez, Harry Kane, Kevin De Bruyne and, most recently, Mohamed Salah.

These were players who did not need to justify their price every two or three gameweeks. Their production, centrality and consistency made going without them an enormous risk.

Today, however, that category appears to be almost empty.

---

## What Is a Premium Player, Really?

The first temptation would be to define a premium by price alone.

But being expensive is not enough.

From my point of view, a premium is the talisman of his team. He pulls the strings, carries leadership and sits at the centre of everything his club does.

He usually has guaranteed minutes, which means we are not constantly worried about whether or not he will start. In Fantasy terms, he also has several routes to points: goals, assists, bonus points, penalties or, depending on the position, a strong chance of keeping clean sheets.

These are players capable of holding a high price throughout an entire season, or at least for long stretches of it.

But their most important characteristic is something else.

They force us to make a structural decision.

Are we going to own them?

Or are we going to accept the risk of going without, suffer the consequences when other managers hand them the captaincy and watch ourselves fall behind from the very beginning?

There are expensive players who are interchangeable. They move in and out of our squads relatively easily depending on fixtures or form.

With a true premium, that move can be far more costly.

> **A true premium shapes your entire squad.**

---

## The Disappearance of the Premium

We could look back at the roles played by Alexis Sánchez, Thierry Henry, Kevin De Bruyne or Mohamed Salah and clearly demonstrate that this was once a very recognisable category.

Players who combined talent, volume, consistency and dominance within their teams.

Now, that profile seems to be disappearing.

It is not that the Premier League has run out of technically gifted players. The problem is that those players no longer provide the same level of certainty in FPL.

Bukayo Saka, for example, can produce like a premium player, but injuries have repeatedly interrupted his continuity.

Cole Palmer showed during his first seasons at Chelsea that he possesses an extraordinary ceiling. He has also struggled physically, which means he has yet to confirm that we can keep him in that premium tier across several seasons.

Phil Foden, at his best, takes a back seat to no one. Yet his role within the team has depended too heavily on the manager, the system and, on many occasions, rotation.

Now, with Enzo Maresca arriving at Manchester City, we will have to see what role Foden is given in the new project. He has just signed a contract extension, and the expectation should be that he receives the leading role his talent demands.

One of the most recent players to reach premium level is Bruno Fernandes.

The Portuguese midfielder delivered a spectacular 2025–26 season. But Bruno is also one of those players who can give you a dominant campaign followed by a disappointing one, without his individual quality necessarily changing.

The team changes.

The players around him change.

His position on the pitch changes.

The managers change.

Michael Carrick may be the solution. He showed signs of that during the closing stages of last season and offers hope for the campaign ahead. Fantasy itself recognised that potential by pricing Bruno at **£12.0m**, placing him directly among the most expensive players in the game.

> **The problem is not knowing whether Bruno Fernandes is a great player. The problem is knowing which version of Bruno Fernandes we are buying.**

A premium, by definition, should reduce uncertainty.

Not increase it.

---

## The Contenders

During a WhatsApp conversation between the members of Bendito Fantasy, our chief analyst, El Profe, identified several players who could form the next generation of premium assets.

I have already mentioned some of them: Saka at **£9.5m**, Palmer at **£9.5m** and Foden at **£7.0m**.

But the list also included Cherki at **£7.5m**, Wirtz at **£7.5m**, Gibbs-White at **£8.0m**, Isak at **£9.0m**, Watkins at **£8.0m** and João Pedro at **£7.5m**.

From a technical standpoint, every one of them has the quality and potential to become the next premium player, but reaching that level requires far more than flashes of brilliance or isolated strong seasons. To join the great names we have been discussing, they must demonstrate consistency over time, proving that their output is not circumstantial but structural to their teams.

FPL itself is already giving us a clue through the prices it has assigned them, signalling that, for now, it does not consider them established premiums. To make that leap, they will need to stay healthy, be trusted and used correctly by their managers, and, in some cases, wait for their clubs to evolve into more competitive sides without losing their place through rotation.

Ultimately, they must become indispensable figures within their teams, assume the attacking responsibility we expect from players in this category, and translate all that potential into sustained production across an entire season. Until that happens, they remain contenders rather than certainties, and none of them enters the campaign with the historical authority that players like Salah, Kane or De Bruyne commanded at their peak.

> **There is no shortage of expensive players. What is missing are unavoidable players.**

---

## And Then the Android Arrives

The player who breaks records wherever he goes.

This time, he broke the market.

Erling Haaland begins the season priced at **£15.5m**, the highest price ever assigned to a player in Fantasy Premier League history.

Paying that much for a forward sounds excessive. But we are talking about the best striker in the Premier League and the final player who still appears to possess the traditional characteristics of a premium.

We also know the Fantasy Towers do not set these prices in a vacuum. They are trying to manage player ownership, preserve the difficulty of the game and control how managers distribute their budgets.

With Salah gone and no other fully established premium available, keeping Haaland at a more accessible price, such as the **£14.0m** he started at last season, would probably have made him too easy to own.

The game would have risked beginning with an almost mandatory squad structure: Haaland as the immovable centrepiece, surrounded by a collection of mid-priced midfielders.

In a sense, Fantasy is not charging us only for Haaland.

> **It is charging us for the absence of another Haaland.**

And even so, the first drafts are already appearing and many managers have included him.

At the time of writing, Haaland is, by a considerable margin, the most-owned player in the entire game, with **72.9% ownership**.

Not even a £15.5m price tag could prevent the last giant from becoming the first player in our squads.

What about you?

Have you already included him?

Or are you going to try to swim against the tide?
